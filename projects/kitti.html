Finding cars in a laser scan

A self-driving car's LiDAR returns a cloud of points. This PointPillars-style detector turns it into 3D boxes around cars. Everything here is seen from above, with the car scanning from the left edge.

15.6×growth in near-range detection score over training
16×fewer candidate boxes after fixing the model head
140training epochs on 850 real KITTI scenes
~$10total cloud GPU cost

How it's built

Points are grouped into vertical columns on a ground grid, a small network summarizes each column, and the result is laid out as a 2D image so a regular image network can process it. For every candidate box the model predicts a score and small corrections to position, size and heading. Overlap between boxes is measured exactly in 3D, and results are reported separately for near, mid-range and far objects.

Python · PyTorch · NumPy · AWS EC2 (T4 GPU) · Google Colab

Honest scope

Trained on 850 scenes without the heavy augmentation published systems use, and scored with a loose overlap threshold and distance buckets rather than the official KITTI protocol. The main result is a working, debugged pipeline on a small budget, not a leaderboard score.

Scan shown is a simplified illustration; numbers come from training on real KITTI data.
